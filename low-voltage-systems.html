Cabling & infrastructure

Good infrastructure makes troubleshooting easier later.

A cable may look simple, but poor routing, weak terminations, mixed power and data paths, damaged connectors or unlabelled runs can create recurring faults that are difficult to trace. We approach low-voltage work as part of the wider system rather than treating each cable as an isolated job.

For new installations, the goal is clean routing, practical equipment placement and enough allowance for maintenance or expansion. For existing sites, we can trace faults, reorganize problematic runs and identify where damaged cables, ports or power injectors are affecting service.

Low-voltage services we support

  • Structured Ethernet cabling and termination support
  • PoE cabling for access points, CCTV cameras and wireless radios
  • Intercom-related low-voltage cabling
  • Signal or network booster installation support
  • Device relocation and cable rerouting
  • Switch, patch and endpoint connectivity checks
  • Fault tracing for cable, port and power problems
  • Cable organization and practical labeling

PoE connects power and data planning.

Power over Ethernet can simplify deployment of cameras, access points and wireless radios, but only when the switch, injector, cable quality and device requirements are compatible. Incorrect PoE assumptions can lead to unstable devices, repeated reboots or complete loss of service. Where PoE is part of a larger network, we coordinate it with our network infrastructure and MikroTik and Wi-Fi work.

Cabling is also part of CCTV and internet distribution.

Surveillance systems depend on reliable cable paths between cameras, recorders, switches and power sources. Internet distribution may require Ethernet runs, access points or radio links between different parts of a property. Explore our CCTV services and Starlink and internet distribution pages for the connected systems that use this infrastructure.

Planning a new run or fixing an old one?

Send the location, the equipment being connected and the approximate distance involved. For troubleshooting, include what is failing and whether the problem is constant or intermittent. This helps determine whether the issue is likely to be cable, power, network configuration or endpoint equipment.
