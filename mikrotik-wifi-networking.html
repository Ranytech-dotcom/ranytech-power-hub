RouterOS & wireless networking

MikroTik works best when the design matches the network.

MikroTik routers are flexible enough to handle simple internet sharing or more advanced business networks, but the configuration should reflect the actual environment. We look at the number of users, internet links, access points, staff/guest separation, remote-access needs and critical devices before deciding how RouterOS should be structured.

MikroTik services we can help with

  • RouterOS interface, IP, DHCP and routing configuration
  • Hotspot and user-access management for controlled internet use
  • VLAN design for staff, guest, CCTV and operational networks
  • Firewall rules and basic traffic-control policies
  • WireGuard, IPsec or other supported VPN connectivity
  • Dual-WAN failover and practical multi-link internet management
  • Bandwidth management where shared links need fairer usage
  • Fault diagnosis for unstable or misconfigured MikroTik networks

Hotspot and guest Wi-Fi access

Guest or shared internet environments often need more control than a normal Wi-Fi password provides. Depending on the requirement, a MikroTik hotspot can support user authentication, session control, device limits and usage policies. The goal is to keep guest access separate from important business systems while still making the connection easy to use.

VLANs help keep different network roles organized.

Staff computers, guest devices, CCTV cameras and management equipment do not always need to sit on the same broadcast network. VLANs can separate these roles and make troubleshooting, access control and future expansion easier. For wider cabling, switching and LAN design, see our network and IT infrastructure service.

Failover for more reliable internet

Where two suitable internet links are available, RouterOS can be configured so a secondary link takes over when the primary one fails. The exact method depends on the providers, addressing and desired behaviour. For Starlink integration and wider internet distribution, visit our Starlink installation page.

Wi-Fi performance is more than router configuration.

Poor Wi-Fi may come from weak signal, interference, overloaded access points, bad placement, backhaul problems or incorrect channel choices. We review both the RouterOS side and the wireless environment so the fix addresses the real cause rather than only changing settings.

Before requesting support

Send the MikroTik model, number of internet links, approximate number of users, access-point brands and a short description of the problem or goal. Screenshots of current RouterOS settings can be useful, but do not send passwords or secret keys.
