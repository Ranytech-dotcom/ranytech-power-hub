Business networking

A good network should be designed around users, devices and traffic.

Reliable connectivity is more than connecting a router to a switch. Business networks often need to support staff devices, guest Wi-Fi, CCTV, printers, servers, access points, IP phones and internet links at the same time. We review how these devices should communicate, which traffic should be separated and where coverage or cabling weaknesses are likely to cause problems.

Core network services

  • LAN planning, Ethernet cabling and structured network distribution
  • Router and managed-switch configuration
  • VLAN segmentation for staff, guest, CCTV and operational traffic
  • Wi-Fi access-point placement and coverage optimization
  • IP addressing, DHCP and conflict troubleshooting
  • PoE planning for access points, cameras and wireless radios
  • Multi-building and point-to-point connectivity planning
  • Fault isolation for unstable, slow or intermittently unavailable networks

Why segmentation matters in a business environment

Putting every device on one flat network can make troubleshooting difficult and can expose operational systems to unnecessary traffic. Where the equipment supports it, VLANs can separate guest users from staff devices, CCTV, servers or other business systems while still allowing controlled access where required.

For MikroTik-specific routing, hotspots, VPNs and failover, see our MikroTik and Wi-Fi networking service.

Wi-Fi should be planned for coverage and capacity.

A strong signal in one room does not mean the whole building has good Wi-Fi. Walls, floor layout, user density, interference and access-point placement all affect performance. We can help identify weak zones, improve access-point placement and reduce the common problem of users connecting to an access point that is too far away.

Internet distribution beyond one building

Where a business needs to extend connectivity between buildings or separate areas, wireless radio links or structured cabling may be appropriate depending on distance, line of sight and site conditions. Our Starlink and internet distribution page covers satellite internet, point-to-point links and wider connectivity support.

Network power and security infrastructure

Routers, switches, access points and CCTV recorders are often affected by poor power quality or repeated outages. If backup power is part of the requirement, see our inverter and battery and solar services. For surveillance networks, visit CCTV and security systems.

Before requesting a site assessment

Tell us the type of property, number of floors or buildings, approximate number of users, internet source and any existing routers, switches or access points. If the problem is a fault rather than a new installation, describe when it happens and which areas or devices are affected.
